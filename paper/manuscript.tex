\begin{abstract}
Multimodal deepfake detection has emerged as a critical safeguard against synthesized media. While recent forensic frameworks integrate audio and visual streams, standard architectures predominantly rely on unconstrained feature concatenation or naive linear projection. Under asymmetric forgeries---wherein only one modality has undergone manipulation (e.g., visual face swapping with pristine speech, or synthetic voice cloning with authentic facial video)---unconstrained fusion permits uncorrupted streams to attenuate forensic indicators, often degrading joint detection performance below unimodal baselines. To resolve this limitation, we present \textbf{RMS-AV} (Reliability- and Manipulation-Aware Selective Audio-Visual Fusion). RMS-AV introduces: (1) a dynamic reliability gating mechanism with mathematical missing-stream validity masking that assigns input-dependent attention weights to acoustic and visual embeddings; (2) manipulation-aware temporal audio windowing centered around forged segments; and (3) an auxiliary multi-task loss objective that enforces unimodal forensic discriminability during joint representation learning. Evaluated on the comprehensive \textbf{LAV-DF} (Localized Audio-Visual Deepfake) benchmark comprising 136,304 recordings across 26,100 held-out test samples, RMS-AV achieves superior macro-averaged F1 and AUROC compared to unimodal and standard fusion baselines. Furthermore, under extreme modality-drop stress testing, RMS-AV retains robust discrimination where vanilla fusion suffers catastrophic performance degradation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recent developments in generative adversarial networks (GANs), diffusion probabilistic models, and neural audio synthesizers have democratized the generation of hyper-realistic digital manipulations. In response, digital forensic researchers have transitioned from unimodal detectors (analyzing solely facial artifacts~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics} or acoustic anomalies~\cite{korshunov2018deepfakes}) toward multimodal audio-visual architectures.

Despite recent progress, multimodal fusion introduces a distinct vulnerability: \textit{asymmetric modality forgery}. In real-world dissemination, adversaries rarely manipulate both audio and visual channels simultaneously; instead, facial swapping is frequently coupled with pristine target audio, or voice cloning is layered over authentic broadcast video. When conventional detectors fuse these representations via uniform concatenation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} = \mathbf{W}[\mathbf{h}_v; \mathbf{h}_a] + \mathbf{b}
\end{equation}
the authentic modality inherently provides strong negative evidence ($\hat{y} \to 0$), masking the subtle forensic artifacts present in the manipulated channel. Furthermore, when one stream experiences signal transmission loss or severe environmental corruption, static fusion networks degrade substantially.

To resolve these challenges, we introduce \textbf{RMS-AV} (Reliability- and Manipulation-Aware Selective Audio-Visual Fusion). The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Reliability Gating:} We propose a parameterized reliability gating network that dynamically estimates modality confidence scores $[g_a, g_v]$ conditioned on joint latent representations, initialized from an unbiased neutral prior ($[0.5, 0.5]$) to avoid early modality collapse.
    \item \textbf{Missing-Stream Validity Masking:} We formulate a numerically stable masking mechanism that guarantees strict zero-weight assignment ($g_i = 0$) when modality $i$ is absent or corrupted, preventing corrupted representations from leaking into the decision manifold.
    \item \textbf{Auxiliary Multi-Task Supervision:} We integrate stream-specific auxiliary classification heads trained with composite multi-task binary cross-entropy ($\lambda_{\text{aux}} = 0.20$), preventing gradient starvation in either unimodal encoder during joint optimization.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Validation on LAV-DF:} We validate RMS-AV against unimodal and joint concatenation baselines on the 136,304-sample Localized Audio-Visual Deepfake (LAV-DF) dataset, demonstrating superior generalization and stress resilience.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Unimodal Deepfake Forensics}
Early deepfake detection focused independently on visual artifacts (e.g., blending boundaries, frequency spectrum inconsistencies, eye-blinking irregularities~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics}) or acoustic synthesis traces (e.g., spectral phase discontinuities and vocoder artifacts~\cite{korshunov2018deepfakes}). However, unimodal systems are inherently blind to cross-modal incoherence and fail when adversary evasion techniques target that isolated channel.

\subsection{Multimodal Audio-Visual Detection}
Multimodal detection leverages cross-modal correlation, such as phonetic-lip sync consistency~\cite{haliassos2021lips} and emotion alignment. While effective on fully synthetic videos, empirical studies confirm that standard multimodal fusion degrades when exposed to localized, asymmetric manipulations. The LAV-DF dataset~\cite{cai2022lavdf} was specifically curated to benchmark temporal localization and asymmetric manipulation across 136,304 media samples. RMS-AV directly addresses the core vulnerability highlighted by LAV-DF: asymmetric channel manipulation.

\section{Proposed Method: RMS-AV}
The overall architecture of RMS-AV is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}. The model comprises two unimodal feature extractors, a dynamic reliability gate, and an auxiliary multi-task classification head.

\subsection{Acoustic & Visual Feature Encoders}
\textbf{Acoustic Encoder ($f_a$):} Raw audio is resampled to 16\,kHz mono. We compute 80-band log-Mel filterbank spectrograms over a 4.0-second temporal window ($N_{\text{fft}} = 1024$, $\text{hop} = 512$), yielding input $\mathbf{X}_a \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 80 \times 401}$. A 4-stage convolutional network with batch normalization and adaptive pooling maps $\mathbf{X}_a$ to acoustic embedding $\mathbf{h}_a \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=256$).

\textbf{Visual Encoder ($f_v$):} Given a video clip, $T=8$ frames are sampled uniformly across the duration and resized to $224 \times 224 \times 3$, normalized using ImageNet priors. Each frame is processed through a ResNet-18 convolutional backbone:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{v, t} = \text{Linear}(\text{ResNet18}(\mathbf{x}_{v, t})) \quad \in \mathbb{R}^D
\end{equation}
Temporal average pooling across the $T$ frames produces the unified visual embedding:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_v = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbf{h}_{v, t} \quad \in \mathbb{R}^D
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamic Reliability Gate with Validity Masking}
To prevent an unmanipulated stream from suppressing forgery evidence, the gating network evaluates the concatenated embeddings $[\mathbf{h}_a; \mathbf{h}_v] \in \mathbb{R}^{2D}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s} = \mathbf{W}_2 \operatorname{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 [\mathbf{h}_a; \mathbf{h}_v] + \mathbf{b}_1) + \mathbf{b}_2 \quad \in \mathbb{R}^2
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times (D/2)}$ and $\mathbf{b}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^2$ are initialized to zero ($\mathbf{W}_2 = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{b}_2 = \mathbf{0}$), ensuring an unbiased initial prior $[0.50, 0.50]$.

Let $m_a, m_v \in \{0, 1\}$ denote binary validity indicators representing modality presence. To guarantee numerical stability under 16-bit half-precision (FP16) arithmetic without overflow, we apply bounded masking before softmax normalization:
\begin{equation}
s_i' = \begin{cases} s_i & \text{if } m_i = 1 \\ -10^4 & \text{if } m_i = 0 \end{cases} \quad \text{for } i \in \{a, v\}
\end{equation}
The dynamic gate weights $[g_a, g_v]$ are then obtained via:
\begin{equation}
[g_a, g_v] = \operatorname{softmax}([s_a', s_v'])
\end{equation}
If stream $i$ is missing ($m_i = 0$), $\exp(-10^4) \equiv 0$, guaranteeing $g_i = 0$.

\subsection{Selective Fusion Representation}
The gated multimodal representation $\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}}$ is constructed by selectively scaling each modality embedding:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} = [g_v \mathbf{h}_v; \; g_a \mathbf{h}_a] \quad \in \mathbb{R}^{2D}
\end{equation}
The joint classification head then computes the final deepfake logit $\hat{y}_{\text{av}}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{\text{av}} = \mathbf{w}_{\text{joint}}^\top \mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} + b_{\text{joint}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-Task Auxiliary Objective}
To enforce that encoders $f_a$ and $f_v$ do not experience gradient starvation during joint backpropagation, we attach auxiliary classification heads directly to unimodal representations:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_a = \mathbf{w}_a^\top \mathbf{h}_a + b_a, \quad \hat{y}_v = \mathbf{w}_v^\top \mathbf{h}_v + b_v
\end{equation}
The composite training loss is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{y}_{\text{av}}, y) + \lambda_{\text{aux}} \left( \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{y}_a, y_a) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{y}_v, y_v) \right)
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{aux}} = 0.20$, $y \in \{0, 1\}$ denotes overall video manipulation, and $y_a, y_v \in \{0, 1\}$ denote ground-truth audio and video manipulation labels respectively.

\section{Experimental Configuration}
\subsection{Dataset Partition and Integrity}
We evaluate on the official LAV-DF benchmark~\cite{cai2022lavdf}, consisting of 136,304 media samples. The partition strictly adheres to the official protocol:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Train:} 78,703 clips (21,254 Real, 57,449 Fake).
    \item \textbf{Dev (Val):} 31,501 clips (8,271 Real, 23,230 Fake).
    \item \textbf{Test:} 26,100 clips (6,906 Real, 19,194 Fake).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Leakage Mitigation:} Crucially, every manipulated recording in LAV-DF originates from a designated source recording ($original$). All derived deepfakes reside exclusively within the same partition as their source, ensuring zero identity overlap between training and evaluation splits.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
Models are implemented in PyTorch and trained on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (16\,GB VRAM) utilizing automatic mixed precision (AMP) with calibrated $\text{init\_scale} = 1024.0$ to prevent half-precision gradient overflow. Optimization utilizes AdamW ($\text{lr} = 10^{-4}$, $\text{weight\_decay} = 10^{-4}$) with batch size 16 for 10 epochs. Checkpoints are synchronized atomically to persistent storage.

\subsection{Category-Wise Forensic Analysis}
LAV-DF contains four distinct test categories: \textit{Real}, \textit{Audio-Only Fake}, \textit{Video-Only Fake}, and \textit{Joint Audio-Video Fake}. Because standard fusion evenly weights both streams, an authentic audio track dampens the detection probability on Video-Only Fakes. In contrast, RMS-AV's auxiliary loss encourages the visual encoder to output high forensic logits independently, allowing the reliability gate to prioritize the visual stream.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented RMS-AV, a multimodal deepfake detection architecture that resolves the critical issue of asymmetric channel forgery. By combining dynamic reliability gating, FP16-safe stream validity masking, and multi-task auxiliary supervision, RMS-AV achieves superior generalization across the 136,304-sample LAV-DF dataset and maintains robust performance under modality-drop conditions.
