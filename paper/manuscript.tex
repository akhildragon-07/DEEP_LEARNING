\begin{abstract}
Multimodal deepfake detection has emerged as an indispensable defense against hyper-realistic synthetic media. While modern forensic architectures process both acoustic and visual channels, conventional models predominantly rely on static feature concatenation or unconstrained linear projections. Under asymmetric forgeries---wherein an adversary manipulates solely one modality (e.g., visual facial replacement accompanied by pristine audio, or neural voice synthesis overlaid onto uncorrupted broadcast video)---static fusion allows pristine streams to attenuate forensic indicators, often degrading joint detection accuracy below unimodal baselines. To overcome this fundamental limitation, we propose \textbf{RMS-AV} (Reliability- and Manipulation-Aware Selective Audio-Visual Fusion). RMS-AV introduces: (1) an asymmetric reliability gating mechanism with half-precision floating-point (FP16) safe missing-stream masking that dynamically modulates acoustic and visual feature contributions conditioned on cross-modal latent interactions; (2) manipulation-aware temporal audio windowing centered around forged speech segments; and (3) a multi-task auxiliary supervision objective that preserves unimodal forensic gradient flow during joint representation learning. Evaluated on the standardized \textbf{LAV-DF} benchmark consisting of 136,304 media samples across 26,100 held-out test recordings, RMS-AV achieves state-of-the-art performance with 81.80\% accuracy, 69.82\% Macro-F1, and an AUROC of 0.9674. Under extreme modality-drop stress testing simulating sensor failure or channel loss, RMS-AV retains 73.6\% of its clean Macro-F1 score, whereas conventional fusion models suffer catastrophic performance degradation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recent developments in generative adversarial networks (GANs), latent diffusion models, and neural speech synthesis algorithms have enabled the generation of hyper-realistic digital forgeries~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics,korshunov2018deepfakes}. Synthetic videos depicting public figures, judicial witnesses, and digital banking users pose unprecedented threats to information integrity, social cohesion, and biometric authentication infrastructure. 

In response, digital forensic methodologies have rapidly evolved. Early detection frameworks operated exclusively on unimodal signals, searching either for facial spatial artifacts (e.g., blending boundaries, color discrepancies, irregular pupil reflections~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics}) or acoustic synthesis traces (e.g., vocoder phase discontinuities, spectral tilt anomalies~\cite{korshunov2018deepfakes}). However, unimodal forensic architectures are inherently blind to cross-modal incoherence and remain susceptible to domain-specific evasion tactics. Consequently, researchers transitioned toward multimodal audio-visual architectures capable of cross-verifying biometric synchrony~\cite{haliassos2021lips,cai2022lavdf}.

Despite their conceptual promise, multimodal architectures introduce a severe, under-addressed vulnerability: \textit{asymmetric modality forgery}. In natural dissemination campaigns, adversaries rarely synthesize both speech and facial tracks simultaneously; rather, visual face swaps are commonly combined with genuine speech, or synthetic voice clones are spliced over unedited television footage. When conventional detectors fuse these representations via uniform feature concatenation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} = \mathbf{W}[\mathbf{h}_v; \mathbf{h}_a] + \mathbf{b}
\end{equation}
the unmanipulated, authentic stream inherently provides strong negative evidence ($\hat{y} \to 0$). This attenuates the localized forensic cues present in the manipulated channel, causing standard fusion architectures to exhibit higher false-negative rates on asymmetric deepfakes than unimodal detectors. Furthermore, in practical deployment scenarios characterized by network packet drops, sensor occlusion, or microphone failure, static concatenation architectures experience total decision collapse.

To resolve these fundamental challenges, we introduce \textbf{RMS-AV} (Reliability- and Manipulation-Aware Selective Audio-Visual Fusion). The primary contributions of this paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Reliability Gating:} We design a parameterized gating network that dynamically estimates modality confidence scores $[g_v, g_a]$ conditioned on cross-modal latent interactions, initialized with zero weights and biases to guarantee an unbiased neutral prior ($[0.50, 0.50]$) at the onset of training.
    \item \textbf{FP16-Safe Stream Masking:} We formulate a numerically robust missing-stream masking mechanism that enforces strict mathematical nullification ($g_i = 0$) when modality $i$ is absent or corrupted, eliminating gradient explosion and half-precision overflow under 16-bit automatic mixed precision (AMP).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Task Auxiliary Supervision:} We integrate stream-specific auxiliary classification heads trained with composite multi-task binary cross-entropy ($\lambda_{\text{aux}} = 0.20$), guaranteeing uninterrupted unimodal gradient flow and preventing modal collapse during joint optimization.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Evaluation on LAV-DF:} We validate RMS-AV against unimodal and joint concatenation baselines on the standardized 136,304-recording Localized Audio-Visual Deepfake (LAV-DF) benchmark~\cite{cai2022lavdf}, achieving an AUROC of 0.9674 and exceptional stress retention under complete stream omission.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Unimodal Deepfake Forensics}
Unimodal forensic detectors focus independently on visual or auditory artifacts. Visual detectors exploit facial blending boundaries, warped facial geometry, texture irregularities, and temporal blink inconsistency~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics}. However, state-of-the-art diffusion-based facial synthesizers increasingly generate artifact-free frames, diminishing the efficacy of spatial-only models. Acoustic detectors focus on spectral unnaturalness and vocoder synthesis traces~\cite{korshunov2018deepfakes}. Nevertheless, acoustic detectors are powerless against visual-only deepfakes such as identity swaps and facial reenactments.

\subsection{Multimodal Audio-Visual Detection}
Multimodal forensic research explores biometric correlation across channels, including phonetic-lip sync consistency~\cite{haliassos2021lips} and affective speech-expression alignment. While effective on fully synthetic videos, empirical studies confirm that standard fusion degenerates when exposed to asymmetric manipulations. The LAV-DF dataset~\cite{cai2022lavdf} was specifically constructed to benchmark temporal localization and asymmetric manipulation across 136,304 media samples. RMS-AV directly addresses the core vulnerability highlighted by LAV-DF: asymmetric channel manipulation.

\section{Proposed Method: RMS-AV}
The overall framework of RMS-AV is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}. The model comprises two unimodal feature extractors, a dynamic reliability gating network, an auxiliary multi-task supervision module, and a joint classification head.

\subsection{Acoustic \& Visual Feature Encoders}
\textbf{Acoustic Feature Extraction ($f_a$):} Given an audio stream, raw signals are resampled to 16\,kHz mono. We compute 80-band log-Mel filterbank spectrograms over a 4.0-second temporal window ($N_{\text{fft}} = 1024$, $\text{hop} = 512$), yielding an input tensor $\mathbf{X}_a \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 80 \times 401}$. To maximize sensitivity to localized audio forgeries, the 4.0\,s window is centered around the detected manipulation period. The spectrogram is processed by a 4-stage convolutional neural network with 2D batch normalization, ReLU activations, and adaptive spatial pooling, projecting $\mathbf{X}_a$ to acoustic embedding $\mathbf{h}_a \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=256$).

\textbf{Visual Feature Extraction ($f_v$):} Given a video sequence, $T=8$ frames are sampled uniformly across the temporal duration and resized to $224 \times 224 \times 3$, normalized using standard ImageNet parameters. Each frame is processed through a ResNet-18 convolutional backbone:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{v, t} = \text{Linear}(\text{ResNet18}(\mathbf{x}_{v, t})) \quad \in \mathbb{R}^D
\end{equation}
Temporal average pooling across the $T$ frame representations yields the unified visual latent embedding:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_v = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbf{h}_{v, t} \quad \in \mathbb{R}^D
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamic Reliability Gate with Validity Masking}
To prevent an unmanipulated stream from suppressing forgery evidence, the gating network evaluates the concatenated embeddings $[\mathbf{h}_a; \mathbf{h}_v] \in \mathbb{R}^{2D}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s} = \mathbf{W}_2 \operatorname{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 [\mathbf{h}_a; \mathbf{h}_v] + \mathbf{b}_1) + \mathbf{b}_2 \quad \in \mathbb{R}^2
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{(D/2) \times 2D}$, $\mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times (D/2)}$, and $\mathbf{b}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^2$ are initialized to zero ($\mathbf{W}_2 = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{b}_2 = \mathbf{0}$), establishing an initial prior of $[0.50, 0.50]$ to prevent premature modality suppression.

Let $m_a, m_v \in \{0, 1\}$ denote binary validity indicators representing modality presence. In standard implementations, invalid channels are masked with large negative values (e.g., $-10^9$). However, under IEEE 754 half-precision floating-point (FP16) arithmetic used in GPU acceleration, the minimum representable normalized value is $-65504$; values below this threshold underflow to $-\infty$ or produce NaN gradients upon backward scaling. To guarantee numerical stability under FP16 training, we apply bounded masking before softmax normalization:
\begin{equation}
s_i' = \begin{cases} s_i & \text{if } m_i = 1 \\ -10^4 & \text{if } m_i = 0 \end{cases} \quad \text{for } i \in \{a, v\}
\end{equation}
The dynamic gate weights $[g_a, g_v]$ are then computed via:
\begin{equation}
[g_a, g_v] = \operatorname{softmax}([s_a', s_v'])
\end{equation}
Since $\exp(-10^4) \equiv 0$ in computer arithmetic, when modality $i$ is absent ($m_i = 0$), its gate weight is strictly $g_i = 0$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{RMS-AV Reliability Gating and Inference}
\label{alg:rmsav}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Audio sample $\mathbf{x}_a$, video frames $\{\mathbf{x}_{v,t}\}_{t=1}^T$, validity flags $m_a, m_v \in \{0, 1\}$.
\ENSURE Deepfake probability $\hat{p} \in [0, 1]$, gate weights $[g_v, g_a]$.
\STATE Extract acoustic embedding: $\mathbf{h}_a \leftarrow f_a(\mathbf{x}_a) \in \mathbb{R}^D$
\STATE Extract visual embedding: $\mathbf{h}_v \leftarrow \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T f_v(\mathbf{x}_{v,t}) \in \mathbb{R}^D$
\STATE Form cross-modal latent state: $\mathbf{z} \leftarrow [\mathbf{h}_a; \mathbf{h}_v] \in \mathbb{R}^{2D}$
\STATE Compute raw gating scores: $\mathbf{s} \leftarrow \mathbf{W}_2 \operatorname{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{z} + \mathbf{b}_1) + \mathbf{b}_2$
\FOR{$i \in \{a, v\}$}
    \IF{$m_i == 1$}
        \STATE $s_i' \leftarrow s_i$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $s_i' \leftarrow -10^4$ \COMMENT{FP16-safe nullification}
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $[g_a, g_v] \leftarrow \operatorname{softmax}([s_a', s_v'])$
\STATE Synthesize gated representation: $\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} \leftarrow [g_v \mathbf{h}_v; g_a \mathbf{h}_a]$
\STATE Compute classification logit: $\hat{y}_{\text{av}} \leftarrow \mathbf{w}_{\text{joint}}^\top \mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} + b_{\text{joint}}$
\STATE Compute deepfake probability: $\hat{p} \leftarrow \sigma(\hat{y}_{\text{av}})$
\RETURN $\hat{p}, [g_v, g_a]$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Selective Fusion Representation}
The gated multimodal representation $\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}}$ is constructed by selectively scaling each modality embedding by its dynamic confidence score:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} = [g_v \mathbf{h}_v; \; g_a \mathbf{h}_a] \quad \in \mathbb{R}^{2D}
\end{equation}
The joint classification head then computes the final deepfake logit $\hat{y}_{\text{av}}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{\text{av}} = \mathbf{w}_{\text{joint}}^\top \mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} + b_{\text{joint}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-Task Auxiliary Objective}
To enforce that encoders $f_a$ and $f_v$ do not suffer gradient starvation during joint optimization, we attach stream-specific auxiliary classification heads directly to unimodal representations:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_a = \mathbf{w}_a^\top \mathbf{h}_a + b_a, \quad \hat{y}_v = \mathbf{w}_v^\top \mathbf{h}_v + b_v
\end{equation}
The composite training loss is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{y}_{\text{av}}, y) + \lambda_{\text{aux}} \left( \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{y}_a, y_a) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{y}_v, y_v) \right)
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{aux}} = 0.20$, $y \in \{0, 1\}$ denotes overall video manipulation, and $y_a, y_v \in \{0, 1\}$ denote ground-truth audio and video manipulation labels respectively. The complete inference procedure is formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:rmsav}.

\section{Experimental Configuration}
\subsection{Dataset Partition and Zero-Leakage Protocol}
We evaluate on the standardized LAV-DF benchmark~\cite{cai2022lavdf}, consisting of 136,304 media samples. The partition strictly adheres to the official benchmark split:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Train:} 78,703 clips (21,254 Real, 57,449 Fake).
    \item \textbf{Dev (Val):} 31,501 clips (8,271 Real, 23,230 Fake).
    \item \textbf{Test:} 26,100 clips (6,906 Real, 19,194 Fake).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Leakage Mitigation:} Crucially, every manipulated recording in LAV-DF originates from a designated source recording ($original$). All derived deepfakes reside exclusively within the same partition as their source, ensuring zero identity overlap between training and evaluation splits.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
RMS-AV is implemented in PyTorch and trained on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (16\,GB VRAM) utilizing automatic mixed precision (AMP). To prevent half-precision gradient overflow in ResNet-18 initial convolutional layers, the gradient scaler is initialized with $\text{init\_scale} = 1024.0$ with gradient norm clipping at $1.0$. Optimization utilizes AdamW ($\text{lr} = 10^{-4}$, $\text{weight\_decay} = 10^{-4}$) with batch size 32. Multi-epoch convergence profiles are depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:training_dynamics}.

\subsection{Modality-Drop Stress Testing}
In practical deployments, streaming channels frequently experience temporary signal dropouts, transmission corruption, or sensor occlusions. To evaluate robustness, we simulate single-stream loss by setting $m_v = 0$ or $m_a = 0$ across the evaluation set. 

As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:stress_test} and illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:stress_test}, standard concatenation fusion collapses when either channel is missing ($\text{Macro-F1}$ drops from $61.20\%$ to $28.40\%$ under video loss and $31.20\%$ under audio loss, representing an average performance drop of $-31.40\%$). In sharp contrast, RMS-AV dynamically redistributes attention to the active stream via FP16 validity masking ($g_a = 1.0$ when $m_v = 0$), retaining \textbf{73.6\%} of its Macro-F1 score under video dropout and \textbf{89.0\%} under audio dropout, limiting overall degradation to $-13.07\%$.

\subsection{Category-Wise Forensic Analysis}
LAV-DF categorizes manipulations into four distinct regimes: \textit{Pristine}, \textit{Visual-Only Forgery} (FaceSwap, Lip-Sync modification), \textit{Acoustic-Only Forgery} (Neural Voice Cloning), and \textit{Joint Forgery}. Fig.~\ref{fig:gate_dynamics} visualizes the empirical gate weights computed by RMS-AV across these scenarios. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item On \textbf{Pristine} inputs, the gate maintains balanced attention ($g_v \approx 0.50, g_a \approx 0.50$), verifying cross-modal consistency.
    \item On \textbf{Visual-Only Forgeries}, the gate dynamically upweights the visual embedding ($g_v = 0.84 \pm 0.05$), preventing pristine audio from diluting facial forgery evidence.
    \item On \textbf{Acoustic-Only Forgeries}, the gate shifts dominance to the acoustic stream ($g_a = 0.87 \pm 0.04$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
We presented RMS-AV, a multimodal deepfake detection architecture that resolves the critical vulnerability of asymmetric modality manipulation. By unifying dynamic reliability gating, FP16-safe stream validity masking, and multi-task auxiliary supervision, RMS-AV achieves state-of-the-art performance on the 136,304-recording LAV-DF benchmark, reaching an AUROC of 0.9674 while delivering exceptional robustness under single-channel failure.
